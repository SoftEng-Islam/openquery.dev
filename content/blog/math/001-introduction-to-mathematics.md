Ask a room full of people which school subject they dreaded most, and watch the hands shoot up for math. If yours is one of them, welcome! You're in very good company, and I'm thrilled you're here. 🎉

Because here's a secret: **you've already done math today.** You worked out how long you had before leaving the house. You checked whether that discount was really a good deal. Maybe you split a bill with friends:

$$
240 \div 4 = 60
$$

That's math! No textbook, no panic, and you probably didn't even notice.

## Math Is Hiding in Your Day

Once you start looking, it's everywhere:

- 🍚 **In the kitchen:** a recipe serves $4$ people and needs $2$ cups of rice. Cooking for $6$? Scale it up: $2 \times \dfrac{6}{4} = 3$ cups.
- ⏰ **On the clock:** you leave at $8{:}15$ and the trip takes $40$ minutes, so you'll arrive at $8{:}55$.
- 💰 **With money:** you have $500$ and spend $130$, which leaves you with $500 - 130 = 370$.

Simple, right? Just addition, subtraction, multiplication, and a little confidence. But this is only the doorway. Math is a *language* for describing patterns and relationships, and once we can describe something precisely, we can measure it, predict it, and build with it. That's why computers run on math, and why scientists use it to describe everything from the tiniest particles to the biggest galaxies. 🔭

So what exactly *is* this thing, and where did it come from? Let's dig in!

## Where Does Math Come From?

Here's a mind-bending thought: objects in the night sky follow mathematical laws of motion whether or not anyone ever writes those laws down. Because of this, many mathematicians believe mathematical truths have a kind of existence of their own, independent of us and our symbols.

Take this one:

$$
1 + 1 = 2
$$

It doesn't depend on two people dropping apples into a basket and agreeing they now have two. It's simply *true*. It was true before any human was born, and it will still be true long after we're gone.

So where does the math from class come from? Did a caveman stumble on a calculus textbook buried under a rock? Was it handed down from the heavens? If math has ever felt like a mysterious thing that everyone else just *gets*, trust me, you're not alone.

The real story is far more exciting. The truths are **discovered**, but the tools are **invented**. The notation, the methods, and the questions we choose to ask all come from people just like us. And this isn't ancient history: right now, thousands of mathematicians around the world are creating brand-new math!

## So, What *Is* Math?

Here's the short version: **math is the study of questions that have definite answers.**

That sounds almost too broad, and it is! To be a bit more precise, these are questions whose answers can be settled by pure logic, with no opinions and no measurements needed. That's exactly what makes math stand apart from every other kind of inquiry.

### Questions without definite answers 🤔

- What is the best form of government?
- Who deserves the Oscar for Best Picture?
- Which is better, pizza or cheeseburgers?

Fun to argue about, aren't they? But these answers are subjective. They change from person to person, and nobody can give a definite answer, because there simply isn't one.

Even science works a little differently. If we repeat an experiment again and again and our equations keep predicting the result, we can be extremely confident we understand what's going on. But we can never be one hundred percent sure the next result won't surprise us. Science can be reliable far beyond reasonable doubt, yet it never claims complete certainty.

### Math plays by different rules ✅

Try this: draw a circle and look at the angle between a radius and a tangent line. You'll find $90^\circ$, or you'll have made a mistake. There's no in-between!

![A circle with center O. The radius OP and the tangent line at P meet at a right angle of 90 degrees.](/img/circle-tangent.jpg)

Every mathematical question like this has a completely clear-cut answer:

- **Are there infinitely many prime numbers?** Yes, definitely.
- **Is there a fraction that, multiplied by itself, gives exactly two?** In symbols, is there a fraction $\dfrac{a}{b}$ with
  $$\left(\dfrac{a}{b}\right)^2 = 2\,?$$
  No, definitely not. We'll see why together in this course!

A mathematician's job is to ask questions like these, then invent the tools to answer them. The answers themselves are fixed, out of our hands, woven into the fabric of reality, and often wonderfully surprising. Is there more than one kind of infinity? **Yes!** In fact, there are infinitely many different kinds of infinity, a staircase that never ends:

   $$
   \aleph_0 \lt \aleph_1 \lt \aleph_2 \lt \cdots
   $$

Humans didn't decide that. We just got curious enough to ask. ✨
